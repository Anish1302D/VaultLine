# VaultLine: Guardrail-First Autonomous Seller Agent for Machine Commerce
**Techfest 2026-27 (IIT Bombay) · AgentX Challenge 2 – Selling to Machines**

### Abstract

As autonomous agents increasingly transact in machine-to-machine commerce, naive large language model (LLM) sellers suffer catastrophic vulnerabilities: susceptibility to prompt injections, authority spoofing, ungrounded arithmetic hallucinations, and severe margin bleed. In baseline testing on official challenge starter bots, unstructured LLM sellers exhibited an unacceptable 60% policy violation rate, frequently selling below acquisition cost or exceeding discount ceilings.

VaultLine addresses these systemic risks through a guardrail-first, 5-layer architecture governed by the foundational axiom: *"Code decides, the LLM only talks."* Commercial authority is strictly removed from generative models and transferred to deterministic mathematical engines bounded by two zero-trust perimeters.

At Trust Boundary 1, the Layer 1 Input Guard intercepts raw buyer messages, strips untrusted prose, clamps negative quantities, and scans for seven adversarial vectors (prompt injection, authority claims, fake competitor quotes, synthetic budget anchors, artificial deadlines, cost probes, and wear-down stalls). It emits a clean, prose-free `BuyerIntent` to Layer 2 (StoreFacts), which computes a dual-constraint floor price with upward ceiling rounding and an epsilon buffer to eliminate floating-point underflow. Layer 3 (Negotiation Engine) computes multi-round concession trajectories via a grid-tuned Boulware curve ($\beta = 1.2$, $\gamma = 0.15$), enforcing reciprocity bonuses, stall freezes on repeated sub-floor bids, volume discounts ($\ge 3, 5, 10$ units), and multi-SKU bundle pricing with independent line-item floor clamping to eliminate cross-subsidies. 

Layer 4 (Composer) serializes approved decisions into wire JSON, embedding policy-grounded deflections for adversarial flags, with optional low-temperature paraphrasing. Finally, Trust Boundary 2 enforces Layer 5 (Output Validator), an independent gatekeeper that audits every outgoing payload against seven mandatory checks: floor clamp, discount ceiling, inventory availability, numerical fact grounding, banned phrase filtering, exact arithmetic invariants ($\text{total} = \text{price} \times \text{qty}$), and strict confidentiality protection (zero leaks of unit cost or secret floors). Any detected violation triggers an automated, deterministic fallback built directly from verified facts.

VaultLine was comprehensively evaluated across 2,100 high-throughput negotiations ($\ge 300$ runs per persona) spanning three starter bots and four adversarial LLM personas (Cooperative, Hard Bargainer, Psychological Manipulator, and Prompt Injector). Across all 2,100 multi-round sessions, VaultLine achieved **zero policy violations** (100% adherence to Hard Constraints HC-1 through HC-8), closed 72.8% of negotiations (1,528 successful transactions), preserved a high mean profit margin of 32.87%, and generated $256,221.27 in cumulative net profit. Furthermore, metamorphic fairness tests (ST-1) verified that identical price sequences wrapped in polite versus hostile, executive versus student, or terse versus verbose prose produce exact cent-for-cent matching counteroffers.

Deployable as an offline Docker container (`VAULTLINE_NO_LLM=1`) with sub-10ms turn latency and zero inference API costs, VaultLine proves that enterprise-grade autonomous negotiation requires deterministic safety architectures rather than prompt-based guardrails. Future extensions will integrate real-time external market indices and cross-currency multi-clearing protocols.
